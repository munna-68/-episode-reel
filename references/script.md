## The template
1. **Hook (≤3 s), over the preloader or hero:** "I built this whole website for a <trade>. <They> never asked me to." Use the owner's pronouns from the brief or their own bio; if unsure, write around them ("…for a local bakery. Nobody asked me to.").
2. **Episode line (≈2.5 s):** "Episode <n>: <Owner> from <Business>."
3. **Problem (≤4 s):** one sentence from the repo's notes (scattered platforms, login walls, a dead site, no booking…). Over a top-zone graphic.
4. **Solution (≈2.5 s):** "So I put everything into one home <they> own(s)." Over the section that best shows "everything in one place".
5. **Feature beats (one line each, ≤4.5 s):** each beat is **one real interaction the camera performs**, said as a benefit to a business owner (more sales, less friction, owning the audience, fewer apps). Order them along the route the take can actually walk: the first click by about 14 s, then nav from page to page. 3 to 5 beats.
6. **Dashboard or back office (if the site has one):** "Behind it all, one dashboard shows <them>…". Demo numbers get a "demo numbers" chip.
7. **Phone (≈2.3 s):** "And all of it works just as smoothly on a phone."
8. **Pitch to owners (≈3.2 s):** "If you run a small business, this is what your website could feel like."
9. **Closing (≈4.7 s):** "<Owner>, let me know what you think. And drop a business below that I should surprise in episode <n+1>."

Target length: 55–70 s. The user is fine going over 60 s when the content earns it, never when it's padding.

## Writing from the repo
- Look for DESIGN_NOTES.md, README.md, `docs/`, or any markdown with "problem", "talking points", "why", or "what's fake". Ep 1's DESIGN_NOTES had a "Talking points for the video" section; use those ideas first.
- Pull the problem and benefits from it. Figures come only from that text or from the site's own copy, and only if they matter. Ep 1 dropped "thirty thousand followers" in the final cut to keep the opening fast.
- Every feature line must name something the take will visibly do. If you can't film it, cut the line.
- Avoid narrating colours, fonts, palettes, easing or "the button rolls its label". The user rejected that as "talking about every small detail".
- Avoid cosy filler in the opening ("I've actually talked to her before", "she is the sweetest person"). It was cut for pace.
- Spell out awkward words for the voice: "the I in KITCHEN" was heard as "iron kitchen", so the fix was "the letter I". Write numbers the way they should be said ("thirty thousand").
